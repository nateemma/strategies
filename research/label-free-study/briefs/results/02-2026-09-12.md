# Brief 02 — Label warm-start + P&L fine-tune: RESULT (2026-09-12)

**Feature**: [`specs/010-labelfree-warmstart-finetune/`](../../../../specs/010-labelfree-warmstart-finetune/)
**Status**: INTERIM — Phase 6 carry-forward runs are gated (no arm survives selection); Phase 7 close pending.
**Verdict: NOT RESOLVED (SC-009).** No arm beats **both** controls by the floor. Direction recorded below:
against the P&L control labels **helped**; against the label control they **hurt**.

## Hypothesis

Brief 01 left one ambiguity: was its failure the *objective* (net-of-cost realised return is the
wrong thing to descend) or the *optimisation* (the right objective, started from noise, cannot find
the basin)? Warm-starting the policy from a label-trained classifier and then fine-tuning on P&L
tests the second. Three arms: **A** warm-start then pure P&L fine-tune; **B** a blended objective
(label cross-entropy + P&L, sweeping the blend weight and the label threshold); **C** an auxiliary
*non-directional* target (forward realised volatility) alongside the P&L term.

**A retraction.** The voided Arm B run produced near-identical numbers across label thresholds and
I reported that as "confirmation the two labels behave the same". It was not — it was the same
panel scored twice. The threshold is now proven live by a per-run assertion that the two panels
differ.

**Two defects worth naming**, because both produced plausible numbers rather than errors:

- `aux_vol_loss` computed `sqrt(var) + EPS`. That is finite in the forward pass, but `d/dx sqrt(x)`
  is infinite at zero, so the *gradient* was NaN. Fixed by moving EPS inside the sqrt; the
  regression test asserts the gradient, not the loss.
- `label_threshold` being inert is the second instance in this study of a swept parameter that
  changed nothing with no error raised.

## Persistence — fold f07 (rising, buy & hold +0.5279 log / +69.5%)

f08 is a bear fold, so Arm C's advantage could have been an artifact of a falling market. It is not.

| | f07 (rising) | f08 (falling) |
|---|---|---|
| buy & hold | +0.5279 | −0.4255 |
| P&L control | −0.5257 | −1.0894 |
| **Arm C** | **+0.0934** | **−0.1083** |
| DO NOTHING | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| paired delta (C − control) | **+0.6191** | **+0.7573** |
| seeds positive | 4/5 | 5/5 |
| bootstrap 95% CI | [+0.2723, +0.9366] | [+0.4060, +1.0148] |

The deltas are statistically indistinguishable and both exclude zero. **The advantage is
regime-independent.**

**But the persistence test devalues the finding rather than supporting it.** Read Arm C's own
return instead of what it beats:

- vs DO NOTHING: +0.0934 (f07), −0.1083 (f08) — across both folds **−0.0075**, a wash.
- vs buy & hold in f07: **−0.4345**. It captured 9.8% of a 69.5% rally.

Two diagnostics explain it. Arm C's across-seed dispersion in f07 is **sd = 0.0345** against the
control's **0.4489** — a 13× collapse. And its mean exposure barely moves between folds (0.369
rising, 0.434 falling) while the market swings 104 points. It is not taking a direction; it holds a
small, near-constant exposure regardless of regime.

**So Arm C's edge over the P&L control is variance reduction, not alpha.** The +0.6 to +0.8 paired
delta is almost entirely the control's losses being *avoided*, not gains being made. The striking
number is the P&L control losing 52% in a +69.5% market — a statement about the control, not about
Arm C.

## Verdict

**NOT RESOLVED.** SC-009 fires: no arm beats both controls by the 3.35 pp/yr floor. Arm C beats the
P&L control decisively and in both regimes, and loses to the label control by 10.6 pp on f08.

**Direction, which SC-009 requires and which is the actual deliverable: labels helped.** Every arm
carrying a label prior beat the pure-P&L control, monotonically in how much prior it carries
(A +0.56, B +0.96, C +0.98). The label control still beats all of them. Brief 01's question is
therefore answered in the *negative*: the failure was **not** merely optimisation. Warm-starting
from the classifier — giving the P&L objective the exact basin it was accused of being unable to
find — recovers only about half the gap, and the recovery does not come from better direction-taking.

## What this changes for the next brief

1. **The pure-P&L objective line is closed.** Three arms, two regimes, and the best of them is
   indistinguishable from holding cash. Do not spend further compute on variants of "descend
   net-of-cost return".
2. **The P&L control is a broken comparator, not a baseline.** Losing 52% in a +69.5% market means
   any arm that merely declines to trade scores a large positive delta against it. Future briefs
   should report against DO NOTHING and buy & hold first, and treat a delta over the P&L control as
   near-meaningless on its own. This report's own headline delta is an instance of the trap.
3. **Brief 03 (meta-labelling from the trade archive) is strengthened.** Labels carrying real
   information is now measured, not assumed — the entire ordering above is driven by label prior
   content.
4. **Brief 04 (RL pooled environment) should be re-scoped or dropped.** It descends the same
   objective from the same starting conditions; brief 01 + 02 together have now failed that
   objective from noise *and* from a warm start.
