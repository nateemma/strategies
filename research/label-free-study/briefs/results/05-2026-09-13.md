# Brief 05 — gradient-free policy search over the exit and guard levers: RESULT (2026-09-13)

**Feature**: [`specs/013-labelfree-es-policy-search/`](../../../../specs/013-labelfree-es-policy-search/)  
**Analyze**: [pre-implementation](../../../../specs/013-labelfree-es-policy-search/analyze.md)  
**Verdict: KILL.** KILL: PBO 0.713 > 0.2 — the measured value IS the finding

## Hypothesis

The levers gradient descent cannot reach — the ATR stop multiplier, `stoploss_grace_*`,
the guard thresholds and minimum holding time — *jointly* matter, but have only ever
been swept one at a time on windows that cannot resolve them. Searching them jointly
against an honest objective may find an operating point one-at-a-time sweeps cannot.

Prior evidence is against it: an entire rvol sweep spans **4.93 pp** inside a **7.633 pp**
noise floor, so the space's total range of effect is smaller than the uncertainty on
measuring it. A quantified negative was the expected deliverable.

## The exit-reason census that shaped the search space

Measured over the 378 archived walk-forward trades, before any code:

| exit reason | trades | share | share of P&L | mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `take_profit` | 195 | 51.6% | +179.7% | `cexit_take_profit` |
| `trailing_stop_loss` | 98 | 25.9% | −120.2% | **the ATR + grace stop stack** |
| `model_exit` | 45 | 11.9% | +21.9% | the model's own signal |
| `unclog_12h` | 27 | 7.1% | +7.6% | the 12h time rule |
| `roi` | 10 | 2.6% | +14.8% | `minimal_roi` |
| `stop_loss` | 1 | 0.3% | −4.7% | the static −0.05 initial stop |

`trailing_stop_loss` is **not** freqtrade's native trailing stop, which is off
(`BaseStrategy.trailing_stop = False`). `interface.py:1654-1658` upgrades a stop exit to
`TRAILING_STOP_LOSS` whenever the stop that fired differs from the initial one, and
`BaseNNStrategy.py:145` sets `use_atr_adaptive_stoploss = True`. **Those 98 exits are the
brief's own ATR + grace stack.** Read naively, the `stop_loss` row alone would say the
headline lever governs 0.3% of exits; it governs 26.2% and the entire loss side.

That is why `cexit_take_profit` was **added** to the search space — 51.6% of exits and
the mechanism that opposes the stop stack — giving ten dimensions, not the brief's eight.

## Why the search "won" and the result is still a kill

CMA-ES was selected in **14 of 15 folds**, so it did beat the random control (SC-009 survives).
That is exactly the outcome the brief warns about: an optimiser can reliably find the best point
in a space whose best point is worth nothing. Beating the control establishes the optimiser
works; it says nothing about whether the space contains an edge.

SC-008 passing beside a PBO of 0.713 is not a contradiction. SC-008 asks whether each of the
**15 selected** configurations collapsed from train to test — none did. PBO asks whether the
ranking over all **23,175** configurations is stable — it is not. A search can pick
configurations that hold up individually while the ranking that produced them is noise, and
that is the multiple-testing story in one line.

## The cost of near-abstention

**9,869 of 48,015 evaluations (20.6%) were inadmissible**, and 19 of the 40 calibration
candidates drawn uniformly from the space were too. Roughly half this parameter space describes
a policy that barely trades. The selected candidates cluster low too — f00 took 4 trades where
production took 13, and f14 took 3 — which is the same pull brief 02 measured, where 92% of an
arm's apparent advantage turned out to be turnover rather than signal.
