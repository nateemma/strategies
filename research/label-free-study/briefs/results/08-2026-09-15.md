## Verdict

**KILL at T032, the gate-activity checkpoint — before any backtest was run.**

The gate fires, but what it blocks is overwhelmingly what the incumbent's `rvol >= 2.0` guard
already rejected, and which bars it blocks depends materially on the random seed. Two of the
three failures say the same thing in different ways: **there is no stable, novel gate here to
evaluate**, so no return or drawdown number from it would mean anything.

Per Principle IV this is recorded as **a defect in the gate, not as evidence about regime
gating**. It is the eleventh instance of the inert-parameter pattern in this repository.

| gate | requirement | measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC-020 | active in ≥ 10 of 15 folds | **13/15** | PASS |
| SC-019 | `novel_block_rate` ≥ 0.20 | **1/15 folds**; mean 0.119 | **FAIL** |
| FR-023 | cross-seed state agreement ≥ 0.95 | **mean 0.916**; 4/15 folds | **FAIL** |
| SC-017 | seed spread < gated-vs-ungated effect | spread **0.115** vs block rate **0.104** | **FAIL** |

SC-017 is the sharpest: **the across-seed spread in the block rate exceeds the block rate
itself.** Changing the random seed moves which trades the gate blocks by more than the gate
moves anything.

## Why the gate is inert: the calibration had nowhere to stand

λ was calibrated to the source study's low-turnover property, targeting **6 regime switches a
year**. The achievable non-degenerate optimum was **1.2/yr** — five times slower — and
`guard_bound=True` records why: **8 of 12 grid points are degenerate**, collapsing one state
below 10% occupancy. Every switch rate between 1.2/yr and the target lies inside that
degenerate region.

So the only admissible model is one so persistent it sits in the low-volatility state almost
permanently. Two folds blocked **nothing at all**; three more blocked ~0.1% of bars.

This is brief 06's recorded selection-by-best-calibration failure in a new setting: the
criterion falls monotonically in the tuned parameter and drives the choice to a degenerate
limit. Here the degeneracy guard caught it, which is the only reason the failure is legible.

## Protocol §5 gates — not reached, and why that is correct

Gates 1–6 (pooled return vs floor, persistence, baselines, PBO/DSR, cost stress, reproduction)
were **not evaluated**. The leak test (US2) and the drawdown comparison (US4) were **not run**,
which is the pre-registered consequence of T032 rather than an omission: performance measured
through a gate that is redundant with an existing guard and unstable across seeds is not
evidence about anything. Running 30 backtests to produce an uninterpretable number would have
been the error.

## What was learned

**1. `jumpmodels`' default predictor is the leak.** `JumpModel.predict()` defaults to
`use_viterbi=True` — a backward traceback, so a bar's state depends on bars after it. Only
`predict_online()` is forward-only. The mandatory audit (FR-017) paid for itself on first
contact: reaching for the obvious method would have silently reintroduced exactly the leak this
brief exists to measure.

**2. The smoothed leak is real but SPARSE.** Truncating history moved the smoothed prefix at
only **2–5 of 104 cut points** — the traceback's influence decays backwards. A deletion test
spot-checking three cut points would have passed a leaking sequence and reported no leak. Any
future causality test in this study must scan a dense grid and assert "changes somewhere".

**3. An end-to-end cross-check threshold is the wrong instrument.** Matched to their
conventions, the two implementations agreed at 0.8935 independently but **0.9708 given the same
weights and centroids** — the inference recursion is right, and the residual is which local
optimum the solver lands in. FR-016 was split into a hard gate on matched-parameter agreement
and an advisory report on independent agreement.

**4. The sparse weighting is bimodal, and the two modes are different economic gates.** Both
solvers hit the identical L1 budget (1.725 = κ) and both zero `sign_persistence`, but one loads
the pair's own volatility (0.752/0.606) and the other loads BTC's (0.747/0.609) — near mirror
images. With `n_init=1`, the same seed on the same data switched from one to the other.
**"Regime driven by this pair's volatility" and "regime driven by BTC's" are different claims,
and the objective is nearly indifferent between them.** That is the most likely root cause of
the seed instability in FR-023, and it is a property of the method, not of this implementation.

**5. A high jump penalty plus a cold start collapses the fit.** Before warm-starting from the
unpenalised clustering, five seeds gave high-volatility shares of
`[0.004, 0.999, 0.996, 0.004, 0.005]` — the model flipping between "everything calm" and
"everything turbulent" on the seed alone. Diagnosed and fixed; the residual instability
(item 4) survived the fix.

**6. Single-seed diagnostics hide seed-dependent degeneracy.** The degeneracy guard originally
ran at seed 0, read a healthy 0.18 occupancy, and passed a configuration that collapsed at three
of five seeds.

## Consequence for the study

`regime/VERDICT.md` returned FAIL over eight regime instruments. This brief adds a ninth by a
mechanically different route and reaches the same place — but with a sharper diagnosis than
"no effect detected".

Parsing all 32 rows of that verdict's evidence table (2026-09-15): **18 rows, 56%, had
`n_taken == n_marginal`** — the gate admitted every marginal trade — all at `edge_pct` exactly
0, with one `beats_bar True` in the whole table. **Over half of Phase C's evidence was gates
that never fired**, and this brief has now reproduced that outcome with an instrument chosen
specifically because it was different. The common cause is visible in item 4 above: on this
universe the volatility-regime axis is already spoken for by the incumbent's rvol guard.

**No brief in this study produced an adoptable candidate.** That is the result.
