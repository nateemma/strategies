**Verdict: harness ACCEPTED** (SC-001 passed). Stress verdict on the incumbent: **NO VETO**.

## Hypothesis

A policy that clears the pooled-WF floor on real data will either keep its P&L and
drawdown shape on plausible unseen paths — the edge is structural — or collapse, in
which case it was fitted to path-specific features of the 15 folds. This is
**infrastructure, not an alpha source**: no policy is trained on synthetic data, because
a generator cannot add information the ~1.5M real bars lack.

## Why there is one generator and not four

The brief planned adapters sampling coherent sequences from every GAN type. Measured
from the running code, cited by `file:line`, with no model instantiated:

| generator | emits sequences | returns | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TAB_DDPM` | **False** | `(n, 1, num_features) float32 numpy array` | `GANs/df_tabddpm_mlx.py:752` |
| `WGAN` | **False** | `Returns np.ndarray (n, 1, F) — 3D with seq_len` | `GANs/GANInterface.py:383` |
| `CTAB_GAN` | **False** | `For CTAB_GAN / MT_CTAB_GAN: generate(n, class_` | `GANs/GANInterface.py:393` |
| `CGAN` | **True** | `For CGAN: generate(n, one_hot=<np.ndarray (n, ` | `GANs/GANInterface.py:389` |

**This is not a defect.** They are class-conditional *row* samplers built for class
balancing and they do that job. It is a statement about what they cannot be reused
for: a row sampler cannot produce a price *path*, and the policy under test is
path-dependent — stops, grace windows, holding limits and take-profit all depend on
the order bars arrive in.

CGAN is the only sequence-capable type and is unmaintained, so it was excluded by
clarification Q3. That leaves a **stationary block bootstrap** as the sole generator —
which the brief required as a baseline regardless. Roughly three quarters of the
planned work disappeared for minutes of probing.

*FR-016: TabDDPM, WGAN and CTAB-GAN+ were rebuilt 2026-09-10 and docs/GAN_SCORECARD.md predates that. Any figure quoted from the scorecard must be re-verified against current code or labelled historical with its date. This census reads current code and quotes no scorecard figure.*

## Calibration — the Tail-GAN criterion

Compared **surrogate-on-synthetic against surrogate-on-real-panels**: the evaluator is
held constant so only the paths vary. Comparing against the 378 archived *freqtrade*
trades would have conflated synthetic-vs-real paths with the known surrogate-vs-
freqtrade gap (−5.30 ± 23.46 pp), and an unattributable failure is a kill here.

Tolerances **pre-registered** in `research.md` R3 before the first run:

## Reading the stress result

**"NO VETO" is not an endorsement**, and the distribution says more than the verdict does.

Two things are worth taking from it.

**The harness reproduces the incumbent's real figure independently.** Feature 013 measured the
incumbent at **+0.171 pp/yr** on the real pooled test folds; the median over 9,481 resampled
paths is **+0.193 pp/yr**. Those were computed from different data — one from the real fold
panels, the other from ten thousand re-orderings of them — and they agree to 0.02 pp. That is a
consistency check the design did not ask for and passes anyway.

**The incumbent's edge is thin against re-ordering.** The 90% interval is
**[−2.71, +2.75] pp/yr**: it straddles zero, and its whole 5.46 pp width sits around a
**3.35 pp/yr** acceptance floor the incumbent does not clear. **38.6% of plausible orderings of
the same market conditions lose money.**

The formal verdict is NO VETO because the loss-path fraction is below half. That threshold is a
blunt instrument, and the honest reading is the distribution: the incumbent's positive record is
consistent with an edge near zero, and roughly two paths in five would have lost. This is
consistent with brief 05's finding that no configuration in a 48,000-evaluation search moved the
incumbent by more than 0.19 pp/yr — a flat region, measured a second way.

**What this does not say**: that the incumbent is bad, or that it should be changed. The harness
is a veto and it did not veto. It says the record is thin, not that it is wrong.

## Limitations — properties of the method, not discoveries

- **A shared block index preserves cross-pair co-movement at the cost of path
  diversity.** Pairs are resampled with one index, so pairs that crashed together still
  do. Resampling them independently would have understated joint drawdown — and the
  drawdown ratio is a calibration statistic, so the bias would have pointed straight at a
  gate it had to pass.
- **The evaluator's error is inherited.** Stress verdicts use feature 013's exit
  surrogate, whose residual divergence from freqtrade is measured (`roi` 22 against a
  real 13 on f07). A stress verdict is no more accurate than its evaluator.
- **A resampled path cannot contain a market state the data has never shown.** This
  measures orderings of observed conditions, not the true data-generating process.
- **The block length was selected by the shortest that passes, not the best calibrated.** KS
  falls monotonically with block length (0.0252 → 0.0119 across 96…288) because longer blocks
  preserve more real structure — so at the limit, where a block spans the series, calibration
  would be perfect and the harness would resample nothing. Selecting by best calibration drives
  toward that degenerate case, so the rule is the most resampling consistent with passing.
- **5.2% of paths (519 of 10,000) were inadmissible** — the policy never traded on them. They
  are excluded rather than scored as flat, since averaging zeros in would understate both the
  spread and the loss fraction.

## Scope

- The multi-task GAN variants (MT_DDPM, MT_WGAN, MT_CTAB_GAN) were **out of scope** and
  appear nowhere in these results (SC-012).
- No policy was trained on synthetic data (FR-017): **verified** — 0 training entry points found in this feature's modules.
- Protocol §5's gates apply to the **policies this harness vetoes**, not to the harness
  itself (clarification Q1, SC-013). A tool has no annualised return.
