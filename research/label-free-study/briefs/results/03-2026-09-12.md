# Brief 03 — Meta-labelling from the trade archive: RESULT (2026-09-12)

**Feature**: [`specs/011-labelfree-meta-labelling/`](../../../../specs/011-labelfree-meta-labelling/)
**Analyze reports**: [pre-implementation](../../../../specs/011-labelfree-meta-labelling/analyze.md) (clean on re-run)
**Verdict: KILL (SC-009).** Nested out-of-sample AUC **0.4978**, CI [0.4953, 0.5025], against a
0.55 gate. Killed before any P&L stage.

**But the reason is not the one SC-009 anticipates, and the difference is the finding.**

## Hypothesis

A secondary model trained on the realised `profit_abs` of trades the primary strategy actually
took — features available at entry only — can learn *when to trust* the primary signal, raising
net return without touching the primary model.

## Config and seeds

`research/labelfree/metalabel/` at commit `cab446c`. Archive: `regime/wf/pooled_nnnc.json`,
378 trades / 15 folds. Warm-up folds 0–4 (91 trades); evaluation folds 5–14 (287 trades,
2.45 yr). Nested: fold *k* trains only on trades whose **`close_date`** precedes fold *k*'s
`test_start`. LightGBM, capacity pinned for the **first** fold's 91 rows (60 trees, depth 3,
4 leaves, min_child_samples 10). Seeds 1–5. Thresholds {0.40, 0.50, 0.60} as pre-registered.

Features, all at the entry bar: the primary's own three class probabilities, plus
`guard_metric`, `rvol`, `atr_pct`, `bb_width`, `adx`, `volume`, `close`. **Pair identity, pair
group and pair history excluded by construction** so that any gain would necessarily be
within-pair.

## Why the kill happened — and why SC-009's default wording would be false

SC-009 says to record that "entry-time features carry no information about outcome beyond what
the primary model already used". **That is not what was measured.** The information is there.
It does not survive being carried across folds.

Ranking the *same* test trades two ways — by raw `p(buy)` with no training at all, versus by
the nested model trained on earlier folds:

| fold | n_test | raw p(buy) | trained model | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| f05 | 25 | 0.8701 | 0.4351 | −0.4351 |
| f06 | 23 | 0.4554 | 0.4710 | +0.0156 |
| f07 | 58 | 0.7031 | 0.6321 | −0.0710 |
| f08 | 71 | 0.5909 | 0.4836 | −0.1073 |
| f09 | 12 | 0.9000 | 0.9500 | +0.0500 |
| f10 | 7 | 0.5000 | 0.0833 | −0.4167 |
| f11 | 48 | 0.5528 | 0.4210 | −0.1319 |
| f12 | 24 | 0.4316 | 0.6035 | +0.1719 |
| f13 | 16 | 0.7273 | 0.5212 | −0.2061 |
| f14 | 3 | 0.5000 | 0.5000 | +0.0000 |
| **mean** | | **0.6231** | **0.5101** | **−0.1130** |

**Training on earlier folds made the ranking worse in 6 of 10 folds**, and 0.11 AUC worse on
average, than using the primary's raw confidence with no model at all.

So the correct direction is: **the primary's confidence ranks its own trades within a window,
but the mapping from confidence to outcome does not persist across windows.** A model fitted on
history inherits a calibration that the next window has already moved away from — which is
non-stationarity, not absence of information.

The leakage audit's information pass, on the evaluation span:

| feature | AUC | vs permuted |
|---|---|---|
| `%predict_prob_buy` | 0.6344 | +0.1386 |
| `atr_pct` | 0.6098 | +0.1070 |
| `bb_width` | 0.5826 | +0.0870 |

| `adx` | 0.5307 | +0.0390 |
| **`rvol`** | **0.4988** | **+0.0001** |

**`rvol` carries no outcome information at all** — indistinguishable from its own permutation.
This repo has separately established that rvol is the *dominant* guard: the chain rejects ~97%
of candidates and rvol does most of that work. So **rvol decides which trades happen while
predicting nothing about which succeed.** Selection power and outcome information are different
quantities, and the strategy's most load-bearing gate has only the first.

## The per-window refit, tested (2026-09-12)

The first version of this report suggested two escapes from the non-stationarity finding. Both
were checked before proposing them further, per Principle VII — *test trackability before
building adaptivity*. **Neither survives.**

### Escape 1 — refit the secondary model per window

For a refit to help, the confidence→outcome relationship must be **trackable**: this window's
optimum must say something about the next one's.

| quantity | mean | sd | lag-1 corr | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-fold AUC of `p(buy)` | 0.6231 | 0.1687 | **−0.5076** | [−0.925, −0.045] |
| per-fold best cutoff | 0.7424 | 0.0288 | −0.4327 | [−0.892, **+0.391**] |

- **The optimal cutoff is not trackable.** Its lag-1 CI spans zero, so last fold's best cutoff
  carries no usable information about this fold's.
- **The AUC anti-persists** (CI just excludes zero, from 9 pairs — weak). A fold where
  confidence ranks well tends to be followed by one where it ranks badly. A refit would
  systematically chase a relationship that has just reversed: worse than useless, not merely
  useless.

The direct test, applied causally — use the previous fold's best cutoff on this fold, versus a
fixed constant cutoff:

```
trailing-best cutoff   mean win rate 0.7667
constant cutoff        mean win rate 0.7522
adaptive - constant    +0.0146   (adaptive better in 4 of 9 folds)
```

Better in **fewer than half** the folds, for +1.5pp of win rate on a quantity whose per-fold sd
is 17 AUC points. This is the pattern already recorded in
`feedback_dynamic_lookback_rejected.md`: *trailing-best loses to a constant; test trackability
before building adaptivity.* It reproduces here exactly.

### Escape 2 — rank relatively within the current window

**This is not causally implementable, and the 0.6231 figure does not mean what the original
text implied it meant.**

Ranking a window's trades against each other requires knowing the window's *other* trades. Live,
those have not happened yet. The within-fold AUC of 0.6231 is computed over a fold's complete
trade set after the fact — it is a diagnostic quantity, not an achievable edge.

To act on a ranking you need a **cutoff**, and the cutoff is precisely the part that does not
transfer (sd 0.0288, lag-1 CI spanning zero). The ordering being informative and the decision
rule being learnable are different claims, and only the first is supported.

### Consequence

The kill stands and is broader than first written. The measured obstacle is not "we used the
wrong fitting scheme" but **the decision boundary itself is unstable and unpredictable across
windows**, which no refitting scheme built from past windows can repair. A follow-up would need
a genuinely different information source — not a different way of fitting the same one.
