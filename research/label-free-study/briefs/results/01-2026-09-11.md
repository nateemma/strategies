# Brief 01 — Direct policy optimisation: RESULT (2026-09-11)

**Feature**: [`specs/009-labelfree-direct-policy/`](../../../../specs/009-labelfree-direct-policy/)
**Analyze reports**: [pre-implementation](../../../../specs/009-labelfree-direct-policy/analyze.md) · post-implementation appended below
**Verdict: NOT RESOLVED.** The simulator passes to brief 2; this family stops after it (SC-013).

## Hypothesis

A small sequence model emitting long-only exposure, trained by gradient descent on
net-of-cost realised return with a turnover control, will convert more of the available
signal into P&L than the label-trained classifier — because the objective *is* the P&L, it
learns its own operating point, and it sees fees, spread and the fill cap during training.

## Config and seeds

`research/labelfree/` at commit `e07a2b9`. Screening fold **f08** (71 control trades, the
most of any fold; deliberately not f07, which is the most *profitable* — screening on a
favourable fold biases selection toward configurations that only work in benign
conditions). Train 2021-01-01 → 2024-12-20, test 2024-12-21 → 2025-03-21, 10 pairs,
date-aligned with a per-pair validity mask. Grid: 2 objectives × 2 turnover controls × 3
cost scales × 5 seeds = 60 runs. Seeds 1–5 via `MLX_SEED`.

**The central finding is the relationship between the two right-hand columns and the left
one: every configuration that scores well does so by not trading, and every configuration
that actually trades loses money.** The six best-ranked arms all sit below the 0.05
participation floor; the two top-ranked are flat on 100% and 99.9% of bars. The arms that
genuinely take positions lose 7.1% (utility-penalty, mean_w 0.13) to 48% (utility-band,
mean_w 0.33) over the 90-day test range.

This replicated across three independent runs under three different objective
configurations — unguarded (v3), with a mis-scaled participation floor (v4), and with a
corrected one (v5). The guard changed which degenerate arm ranked top; it did not produce a
profitable one.

## Screen resolvability

The screen reports **resolved** (rank1 − rank3 = 0.0032, CI [0.0026, 0.0047], excludes
zero), but that verdict is an artifact and must not be read as a finding: near-flat
policies have tiny, low-variance returns, so they are trivially distinguishable from each
other while all being indistinguishable from doing nothing. The pre-registered
carry-forward rule fired correctly and carried `robust_sharpe-band-g1.0` and
`robust_sharpe-penalty-g1.0` — both degenerate at mean exposure 0.002 and 0.003.

**No configuration was carried to Phase 6.** Putting a 100%-flat policy through the pooled
walk-forward would burn ~4 hours to produce zero trades, and the constitution is explicit
that a zero-trade arm is inconclusive, never a zero-return result.

## Baselines and cost stress

Not run. The four mandatory baselines and the cost-stress sweep (protocol §3, §4) exist to
characterise a *candidate*; there is no candidate. Running them against a policy that does
not trade would produce a table of zeros.

## PBO / DSR

**Not computed — deliberately.** The Deflated Sharpe Ratio and the Probability of Backtest
Overfitting (protocol §4, FR-024) characterise an accepted configuration. No configuration
was accepted, and neither statistic is meaningful here: both ask whether an apparently good
result survives correction for selection, and nothing in this grid was good. PBO in
particular measures whether the best in-sample configuration underperforms out of sample,
so when no configuration is profitable there is nothing to be overfit *to*.

An exploratory CSCV value was computed while writing this report and is **excluded** rather
than quoted: it has no completed task behind it (T043 is unreached), and CSCV is biased low
at small configuration counts — measured on this implementation, a true 0.5 reads 0.148 at
8 configurations and 0.385 at 20, against this grid's 12. A number that is untraceable,
biased and uninformative does not belong in a result.

The trial log retains all 243 pre-registered trials, so whichever brief next produces a
candidate can compute both statistics over the full accumulated count.

## Why the objective did not convert signal into P&L

The robust risk-adjusted objective has a **degenerate global optimum at zero exposure**
whenever nothing is profitable: the risk-adjusted return of a zero-return series is
0/(0+ε) = 0, its worst-window term is also 0, so inaction scores 0 while any real strategy
that loses scores worse. Adding a participation floor removes the trivial optimum but does
not create an edge — forced to participate at γ=0.5, the robust arm goes from −0.0015
(flat) to −0.0145 (trading). That is the honest measurement the degenerate optimum was
concealing, and it is negative.

## What was delivered regardless

The **calibrated simulator** (protocol §1), which four sibling briefs depend on. It
reconciles with the arbiter to **0.00000000 bps** per trade and **0.00000000 pp** total
across 99 trades on W1. That was the feature's blocking gate and its MVP, and it stands
independent of this verdict.

## What this changes for the next brief

1. **The simulator is ready.** Briefs 2, 4, 5 and 6 can consume it without re-deriving
   calibration. Its scope boundary is fixed and documented: it validates the accounting
   layer (fees, fill timing, liquidity cap, stake and portfolio sizing) and deliberately
   does not implement the deployment exit stack.
2. **Any objective containing a risk-adjusted ratio needs a degeneracy guard**, and the
   guard must normalise by the validity mask and survive the cost scale. A penalty routed
   through `mean()` has a per-bar gradient of ~1/T and gets outvoted as γ rises; a hard
   projection would not. Brief 2 inherits this.
3. **Brief 2 (label warm-start → P&L fine-tune) is now the more informative experiment**,
   precisely because it starts from weights that already trade. This result cannot
   distinguish "the P&L objective is wrong" from "gradient descent from random init cannot
   find a trading policy in this signal-to-noise regime" — a warm start separates them.
4. **f08 is a single fold.** This is a screening result, not a pooled walk-forward verdict.
   It is sufficient to decline Phase 6 for these configurations; it is not evidence about
   the family across all 15 folds.
