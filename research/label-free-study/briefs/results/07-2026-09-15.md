# Brief 07 — self-supervised representations as extra features: RESULT (2026-09-15)

**Feature**: [`specs/015-labelfree-ssl-features/`](../../../../specs/015-labelfree-ssl-features/)  
**Analyze**: [both passes](../../../../specs/015-labelfree-ssl-features/analyze.md)  
**Verdict: KILL.** A contrastive self-supervised encoder **cannot carry an unpredictable first-moment target**, which is what a forward return is. The hypothesis fails mechanically, not just empirically.

## The finding: a contrastive objective discards what this brief was looking for

Handed the forward return **directly as an input channel**, the screen did not notice it:

```
ridge on the RAW input (encoder bypassed)   rho 0.9624
ridge on the EMBEDDING                      rho 0.0184
```

Measuring what the encoder retains, by target type:

| target | share of recoverable signal retained |
|---|---|
| predictable target (periodic) | **106%** |
| unpredictable first-moment target, handed as its own channel | **9.5%** |
| iid noise channel | **7.0%** |

**A contrastive objective optimises representation *consistency*, not information**
**retention.** It keeps what is predictable or compressible and drops the rest — including
a first-moment target handed to it directly. A forward return in a near-efficient market
is exactly such a target.

This is the brief's own literature review (§E: *SSL embeddings learn second-moment
structure — volatility, similarity — not conditional mean*) demonstrated mechanistically
in this repository, in minutes, rather than cited.

**Two false starts are recorded, because both were instructive.** The first planted leak
was iid noise, which a contrastive encoder correctly discards — an unfair test that fails
on a sound encoder. The second was a real forward return, structured by overlap (lag-1
autocorrelation 0.839) and still discarded: **autocorrelation is not compressibility**.

## The bar, measured rather than quoted

ρ ≈ 0.15 is cited throughout this repo as the OHLCV information ceiling. It is a
**horizon-3-to-5 number**, not a constant:

| H (bars) | median ρ | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.0352 | 0.0184 | 0.0725 |
| 6 | 0.0461 | 0.0324 | 0.0944 |
| 12 | 0.0396 | 0.0266 | 0.0951 |
| 24 | 0.0328 | -0.0176 | 0.0746 |
| 48 | 0.0238 | -0.0613 | 0.0656 |
| 96 | 0.0401 | -0.0596 | 0.0688 |

Two corrections were needed to get here, both to this feature's own first draft:
it generalised the ceiling from **one fold**, and its probe stacked all pairs then
sliced the array — splitting **by pair rather than by time**, a contemporaneous
cross-sectional split that inflated ρ at long horizons (0.0926 against 0.0490 at
H=48 on f07). Both errors inflated the bar, inside a decision whose entire point was
that the bar must be measured rather than quoted.

**Consequence**: at H=48 the hand-built features reach ρ 0.0499 with a *negative*
minimum. A gate defined as 'beat them there' is nearly 'beat zero', so the primary
was amended — before any encoder was trained — to require passing at **both** H=48
and H=6, where the bar discriminates.

## The screen (confirmatory)

Primary declared **before any combination was scored**: `engineered` at H=48+6.

| combination | embedding | hand-built bar | random control | folds beating bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `engineered@6` | 0.0358 | 0.0461 | 0.0012 | 0/10 |
| `engineered@48` | 0.0326 | 0.0238 | -0.0015 | 3/10 |
| `ohlcv@6` | 0.0194 | 0.0461 | 0.0012 | 0/10 |
| `ohlcv@48` | 0.018 | 0.0238 | -0.0015 | 4/10 |

**Pair-agnosticism** (`AGENT_GUIDE.md:545`): max ratio **0.2608** against a
pre-registered threshold of 0.3, anchored on production's own worst
dimension (0.2297 across four folds). 0 of 320
dimensions exceed it — **PASS**.

## What the encoder adds over its own input — the comparison that isolates it

The screen originally compared `embedding(X)` against the hand-built feature bar. That conflates
**two** differences — the encoder's effect and the input's — and measuring it showed the
conflation is not hypothetical: under a leaked target, raw OHLCV beat the engineered bar and it
was entirely the input's doing. The bar that isolates the encoder is a ridge on **its own raw
input**, same split, same ridge.

| combination | encoder adds (median, paired) | folds where it adds |
|---|---|---|
| `engineered@6` | **−0.0089** | **0 / 10** |
| `engineered@48` | −0.0012 | 5 / 10 |
| `ohlcv@6` | −0.0007 | 4 / 10 |
| `ohlcv@48` | +0.0089 | 7 / 10 |

**The encoder adds nothing.** Three of four combinations are negative, and the fourth sits at the
horizon where everything is noise — the causal bar there is 0.0238 with negative fold minima, and
per-fold `adds` swung between −0.024 and +0.021. `engineered@6`, the combination with the most
signal in its input, is the clearest: the encoder **loses** information in **10 folds out of 10**.

*A note on reading the table above it: those are medians of each column, and a median of
differences is not the difference of medians. The paired per-fold figure here is the meaningful
one, which is why `engineered@48` can show embedding 0.0326 against input 0.0238 while its paired
`adds` is negative.*

**The random control sits at ±0.0015 throughout**, so none of this is capacity.

**Pair-agnosticism**: max ratio **0.2608** against the pre-registered 0.30, 0 of 320 dimensions
over — the embedding would have been deployable. It simply carries nothing worth deploying.

## What this does and does not say

- It does **not** say TS2Vec is broken. The encoder trains, its embeddings are
  non-degenerate and reproducible under a fixed seed, the deletion test shows no
  dependence on later bars, and it retains a predictable target at ~106%.
- It says a **contrastive objective is the wrong tool for a first-moment prediction**
  **target**, and that is a property of the method rather than of this implementation.
- The screen measures **learnability**, which this repo has recorded is not edge — a
  +0.155 MCC once produced identical trades. Passing would have earned a backtest, never
  an acceptance. It did not pass.

## What would change the conclusion

An encoder whose objective rewards *predicting* rather than *representing* — a forecasting
head, a masked-prediction objective — might retain first-moment information. That is a
different brief, and this result is the reason to write it rather than to assume it.

## Scope

- **TS2Vec only.** Kronos-small and Chronos-Bolt were out of scope: neither `transformers`
  nor any pretrained weights are installed, and the retention finding applies to any
  contrastively-trained encoder, so downloading them would likely reproduce it.
- **MLX**, matching the repository's NN stack. No package was installed and no weights
  were downloaded.
