# The OHLCV "information ceiling" — three measurements, two of them wrong

`ρ ≈ 0.15` is cited across this repository as the ceiling on predicting forward returns from
OHLCV (`us_spot_market_study.md:24`). Brief 07 needed it as a comparison bar, so it was
re-measured — three times, because the first two attempts were wrong in ways worth recording.

## Generation 1 — stacked split (WRONG)

```
H        3        6       12       24       48       96
median  0.1717   0.1127   0.0944   0.0720   0.0826   0.0583
```

**Defect**: the probe stacked every pair's features into one array and then sliced 60/40, so
"the first 60%" was **the first six pairs**, not the first 60% of time. A contemporaneous
cross-sectional split, not forward-in-time prediction. Measured on f07 it nearly doubled ρ at
long horizons (0.0926 against 0.0490 at H=48), because market-wide moves leak between fit and
score sets and those dominate as the horizon grows.

It was also generalised from **one fold with six pairs**, inside a decision whose entire point
was that the bar must be measured rather than quoted.

## Generation 2 — per-pair temporal split, leaked target (WRONG)

```
H        3        6       12       24       48       96
median  0.1465   0.1003   0.0684   0.0556   0.0499   0.0254
```

**Defect**: the forward return was `open[t+H] / open[t]`, measured from the bar's own open.
Features at bar `t` are known only at `close[t]`, so the target **included bar t's own move** —
information the inputs contain directly.

The damage, measuring the same thing with the target starting after `close[t]`:

| fold | input | from `open[t]` | from after `close[t]` | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| f05 | ohlcv | 0.2186 | 0.0676 | −69% |
| f07 | ohlcv | 0.2513 | 0.0602 | −76% |
| f09 | ohlcv | 0.1796 | 0.0441 | −75% |
| f05 | engineered | 0.1109 | 0.1007 | −9% |
| f07 | engineered | 0.1566 | 0.0861 | −45% |
| f09 | engineered | 0.0969 | 0.0669 | −31% |

**It inverted a conclusion.** Under the leak, five raw log-return channels appeared to beat the
24-column engineered feature set by **~1.95× at every horizon, 25/25 fold-horizon comparisons**
— a result that looked important enough to propose its own brief. It was an artefact: the raw
channels expressed the overlap most directly. With the target aligned causally, **the engineered
set wins instead.**

**The tell was the stability.** A ratio pinned at 1.87–1.99× across five horizons is not
robustness, it is the signature of a mechanical relationship. That should have prompted the
check before the claim.

## Generation 3 — causally aligned (CURRENT)

A signal formed at bar `t` is known at `close[t]`; the strategy enters at the **next** bar's
open. So the target is `open[t+1] → open[t+1+H]`, and `research/labelfree/ssl/inputs.py::
forward_return` implements exactly that.

```
H        3        6       12       24       48       96
median  0.0352  0.0461  0.0396  0.0328  0.0238  0.0401
min     0.0184  0.0324  0.0266  -0.0176  -0.0613  -0.0596
```

**Two things change, and the second is the larger.**

**The level.** The hand-built features reach **0.024–0.046** under causal alignment, against
0.025–0.147 under the leak. At the short horizons where the leak bit hardest, the bar falls by
about 4x.

**The shape.** The monotone decay with horizon **disappears**. Generations 1 and 2 both showed ρ
falling from ~0.15 at H=3 to ~0.03 at H=96, and that decay was itself an artefact: the leak
contributes bar *t*'s own move to the target, and that contribution is proportionally largest
when the horizon is shortest. Remove it and predictability is roughly **flat and low at every
horizon from 3 to 96 bars**.

This matters beyond brief 07. "Predictability decays with horizon" was stated as a finding twice
in this feature's own planning documents, and the 6-bar screen was justified on it. It does not
survive.

**A limit on what this says about `us_spot_market_study.md`.** Its ρ ≈ 0.15 is not reproducible
here under causal alignment, and it *is* reproducible under the leaked alignment at H=3–6. That
is suggestive, not conclusive: how that study aligned its target is not recorded, so this is
evidence about **this pipeline**, not a refutation of that one. Anyone relying on ρ ≈ 0.15 should
re-derive it before building on it.

Current numbers live in `research/labelfree/ssl/baseline.json`, regenerated by:

```
python -m research.labelfree.ssl.screen --baseline-only --folds f05..f14
```

## What to take from this

- **Do not quote ρ ≈ 0.15 as a constant.** Predictability decays sharply with horizon, so a
  figure measured at one horizon is the wrong bar at another.
- **Do not quote these numbers either.** Re-measure with the pipeline you are comparing through;
  that is what generations 1 and 2 failed to do and what generation 3 does in code.
- **Check the target's alignment before believing any ρ.** Two of the three generations were
  wrong about *when the target starts*, not about the model or the features.
- **Suspiciously stable ratios deserve a causality check**, not admiration.
